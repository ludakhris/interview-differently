    Note and follow-up indicators on the Talent list and the roster

    Staff no longer have to open a person to find out whether anyone left a note.     Support follow-ups: track what helps a person succeed

    Staff often learn that someone needs help with something outside the course: a bus pass for evening sessions, childcare during a workshop, a loaner laptop. A support follow-up is a small, trackable to-do for exactly that, kept with the person so the next instructor sees it too.

    Each follow-up is a card with a bold title, chips for the kind of help, the due date (an amber "Due soon" and a red "Overdue by N days") and who it is assigned to, a short details note, and who added it and when. One Status control (Open, In progress, Resolved, Cancelled) saves immediately. "Add follow-up" and Edit open a dialog, so the title, date and assignee can be changed later. Resolved items fold into a collapsed group so the open ones stay on top.

    Follow-ups show in three places: on the Talent participant page, on each learner's record inside a cohort (with an open count in the page's navigation), and in the provider-wide Support queue, which lists every open item across the provider with filters for status, assignee and due date.

    Good to know
    • Follow-ups are private to provider staff and marked with a lock. Learners never see them, and every read or change is recorded in the access log.
    • Nobody can read follow-ups or notes about themselves.

    My profile: one profile per person, shared on the learner's terms

    Learners now keep a single profile about themselves: resume, education (as many entries as they need), work experience, and salary targets. It is about the person, so it does not change from program to program. The learner chooses which organizations can see it from a list, and nothing is shared until they say so. Staff see the content only for people who shared it with their organization.

    A profile counts as complete when it has an education entry with a level, years of experience, at least one industry, at least one job wanted, and a resume. The side panel shows that checklist as it fills in, with green ticks for what is done and an open circle for what is not. Industries and jobs are typed or tapped as chips, not comma lists. The Save bar stays pinned to the bottom of the screen.

    Good to know
    • Complete now includes a resume, and both industries and jobs. Profiles that had only one of those read as incomplete until the missing item is added.
    • Sharing a profile with an organization also lets that organization introduce the learner to employers it works with. The page says so.
    • Pay is never shown in lists, reports, or logs. Staff reveal it with a separate click, and each reveal is recorded.

    A cohort can require the profile, and a refresh

    On the cohort page, staff can tick "Learners must complete their profile first" and choose how often learners must refresh it (every 3, 6 or 12 months, or never). When it is on, "Your profile" appears as the first item in the learner's course, counts toward completion, and is marked done only while the profile is complete and fresh. When a refresh comes due, the item reopens with a note and My learning shows a small banner.

    Good to know
    • It is off by default; cohorts without it behave exactly as before.
    • It does not lock the rest of the course. It is required for completion, and shown first.
    • Learners still decide whether the organization can read the profile.

    Talent management for staff: notes, support follow-ups, search and export

    Provider staff get a Talent area. They can search and filter participants, open a profile, and keep private notes and support follow-ups (for example a bus pass or childcare) that follow the person across that provider's cohorts. A provider-wide support queue shows open items by status, assignee and due date. Staff can export shared profiles to a spreadsheet, with or without pay.

    Good to know
    • Staff notes are now called "Instructor notes", with a line on why to use them: leave context for whoever works with the person next.
    • Notes and support items are never shown to the learner and never returned by any learner screen.
    • Only provider admins of that provider (and system admins) can read them. Every read or change is recorded in an access log, and nobody can read notes about themselves.
    • A person who did not share their profile shows as "Not shared", with only whether it is complete and up to date.
    • The Talent list says whose people it shows, lists every cohort a person is in (withdrawn ones marked), and explains what Industries, Years, "Not shared" and "None" mean. Switching workspace keeps you on Talent.

    Attendance for live and hybrid cohorts

    Cohorts now say how they meet: online, live, or hybrid, and show it as a chip (💻 Online, 🏫 Live, 🔀 Hybrid) on the cohorts list and the cohort page. Live and hybrid cohorts also carry a 📋 Attendance tracking chip. For live and hybrid cohorts, the cohort page has an Attendance section above the roster. A status strip shows sessions held, the overall rate, the last session, and how many learners are below 75%. Each session is a row with its own buttons: Record attendance (or Edit), View, Download CSV, Edit session and Delete. Recording opens a roomy popup where "Mark everyone present" fills the sheet and you flip only the exceptions with four clear Present, Absent, Late and Excused buttons or the P, A, L and E keys. Every learner row has a visible note field, so notes are entered with the attendance itself.

    "Attendance by learner" is a button that opens a grid styled like the roster, with a colored legend. Each learner's name opens their record.

    Good to know
    • The rate counts only sessions that were actually taken and that the learner was enrolled for; excused sessions are left out.
    • Learners see their own attendance line on the course page, never staff notes. Phones get stacked session cards and a full-height recording sheet.

    Activity logs: active time per learner per day

    For online courses, the learner pages record how long each person is actively working: a session has a start and a duration. Staff open Activity from the cohort header. The report shows the average per learner, the average per active day, how many learners were active, a daily chart, and a learner table. A "Daily log" button on each row, or the learner's name, opens that person's day-by-day sessions. Download CSV exports one row per session.

    Good to know
    • Times are stored in UTC and shown in the viewer's own timezone, and days are counted in that timezone.
    • It counts active time only (page open, visible, with recent input). Learners are told on the course page that their time is recorded.
    • Time inside a connected tool is launch to return, because the tool runs in another window.

    Cohorts, rosters, join codes and course offers

    A cohort runs one course from a start date; the end date follows from the course length. Each cohort gets a join code and a roster with progress. Staff can add a learner by email, withdraw one (results are kept), set a maximum cohort size, and offer a course to organizations under the same agency.

    The learner path: join with a code, learn, take quizzes, build a readiness record

    A learner joins a cohort with its code, then works through the course outline one item at a time: lessons, quizzes and assessments. Quizzes are graded on the server and the answer key is never sent to the browser. Knowledge checks can be retaken and keep the best score; pre and post assessments count once. Progress feeds a readiness record that shows where the learner stands.

    Good to know
    • Work is blocked before a cohort starts and after it ends.

    SCORM packages play inside a course

    Course authors can upload a SCORM 1.2 or 2004 package into a module. Learners play it inside the course, and what the package reports (completion, pass or fail, score, where they left off) is saved per learner and counts toward course completion. Bad packages are refused with a clear message before anything is kept.

    Typed practice interview with scored coaching

    Authors give a practice-interview item a role and up to six questions. The learner types an answer to each, and each answer is scored from 0 to 100 with specific coaching on how to improve. The best score counts toward the readiness record and toward the agency's interview-ready measure.

    Good to know
    • Authors choose how many attempts a learner gets (1 to 5; the default is one).
    • Only scores and feedback are kept, not the learner's answers.
